Bead-on-a-String (Brock String)

Step-by-step clinical examination protocol, patient instructions, diagnostic interpretation, and practical optometry testing pearls.

Equipment Needed: A piece of string, a bead (or a nut), and a fixation point to tie the string to (like a wall or a chair).

I. Purpose and Principles

Clinical Value: This is a highly effective orthoptic exercise used to train fusional convergence and treat convergence insufficiency. It is particularly suitable for patients with quite marked convergence insufficiency, such as those with a Near Point of Convergence (NPC) of 20 cm or greater.

Physiological Basis: The test relies on the principle of physiological diplopia. By forcing the patient to maintain awareness of the string extending in front of and behind the fixated bead, the practitioner can ensure both eyes are being used simultaneously and track precisely where the patient's eyes are converged.

II. Procedure

Thread a bead (or nut) onto the piece of string.

Attach one end of the string to an object directly in front of the patient (e.g., tied to a wall or a chair), or have the patient hold it at arm's length.

The patient holds the other end of the string against the bridge or tip of their nose, ensuring the string is pulled taut and held level.

Initially, place the bead on the string at a distance further away from the patient than their measured near point of convergence.

Instruct the patient to fixate directly on the bead.

Gradually move the bead closer towards the patient's nose (similar to the "push-up" NPC method) until the patient either reports diplopia (seeing two beads) or suppression occurs.

III. Clinical Observation & Interpretation

The "X" Pattern (Normal Response): When the patient fixates successfully on the single bead, they should perceive the string forming an "X" that crosses exactly at the center of the bead.

Monitoring Suppression: The perception of the "X" must be specifically pointed out to the patient. Because the string is seen in physiological diplopia, if the patient reports seeing only one string instead of two crossing strings, they are suppressing one eye.

Convergence Accuracy: If the "X" crosses in front of the bead, the patient is over-converging. If the "X" crosses behind the bead, the patient is under-converging.

IV. Advanced Clinical Pearls

Tactile and Visual Feedback: Because this method provides both a strong visual target (the crossing strings) and a tactile input (holding the string to the nose), it is particularly useful for youngsters who also suffer from hand-eye coordination difficulties.

Warm-Up Exercise: Many practitioners instruct their patients to use the Brock string (or a similar dot card) as a daily "warm-up" exercise before moving on to more complex therapies, such as free-space stereograms.

Therapeutic Goal: The patient should practice crossing the strings directly at the bead and maintaining that single image while the bead is moved progressively closer, aiming to maintain the "X" to within 3 cm of the nose

Clinical Tip Always ensure proper patient positioning, steady fixation target alignment, and clear patient communication before initiating this procedure.
