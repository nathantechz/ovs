Stereopsis Testing (Stereoacuity)

Step-by-step clinical examination protocol, patient instructions, diagnostic interpretation, and practical optometry testing pearls.

1. Purpose of the Test

Evaluate Binocular Cooperation: Stereopsis is considered the most sensitive test of binocular function,. It confirms that the patient has good vision in both eyes, good ocular alignment, and overlapping visual fields.

Measure Depth Perception: It quantifies the patient’s ability to perceive depth based on retinal disparity (the slight difference in the angle of images received by each eye),.

Screening: It helps detect suppression, amblyopia, or strabismus (squint). A lack of stereopsis often suggests a developmental anomaly of vision,.

2. Equipment Required

Stereo Test Booklet: Common types include the Titmus Fly (or Stereo Fly), Randot, or TNO test,.

Polarizing Glasses: Used for Titmus and Randot tests to separate the images seen by the right and left eyes,.

Red-Green Goggles: Used specifically for the TNO test.

Good Lighting: An overhead lamp or good room illumination directed at the booklet,.

3. Detailed Procedures

General Preparation

Correction: The patient must wear their best refractive correction (spectacles or contact lenses). If they are presbyopic (over ~40 years old), they must wear their near correction or look through the reading segment of their bifocals,.

No Occlusion: Ensure both eyes are open. Do not cover one eye.

Positioning: Hold the test booklet at 40 cm (approx. 16 inches) directly in front of the patient,. The plane of the booklet should be parallel to the patient's face.

Lighting: Ensure bright, glare-free illumination falls on the page.

Method A: Titmus Fly / Randot Test (Polaroid Method)

This is the most common clinical test using polarized glasses.

Goggles: Place the polarized glasses on the patient (over their prescription glasses if worn).

Gross Stereopsis (The Fly):

Direct the patient’s attention to the large house fly image.

Instruction: Ask the patient to "pinch the wings of the fly",.

Observation: If stereopsis is present, the wings will appear to float above the page. The patient should try to pinch the air above the book. If they touch the paper, they likely do not see the 3D effect (or have very poor stereopsis),.

Fine Stereopsis (The Circles/Wirt Rings):

Direct attention to the graded circles (usually numbered 1 to 9 or 10). Each set contains 3 or 4 circles.

Instruction: Ask, "Which one of these circles seems to float forward?" or "Which circle is the odd one out?",.

Process: Continue until the patient makes two consecutive mistakes. The last correct answer indicates their threshold,.

Note: The disparity becomes finer (harder to see) as the numbers increase (e.g., #1 is 800 seconds of arc; #9 is 40 seconds of arc),.

Animals (Children):

For children, use the rows of animals. Ask, "Which animal looks closer to you?",.

Method B: TNO Test (Anaglyph Method)

Useful because it has no monocular cues (you can't "cheat" by looking for off-center circles).

Goggles: Place the red-green goggles on the patient.

Screening Plates (I-III):

Show the plates containing butterflies or geometric shapes.

Instruction: "How many butterflies can you find?" or "Point to the shapes." These are visible only with red-green glasses if binocularity is present,.

Quantitative Plates (V-VII):

These plates show circles with a "slice" missing (like a Pac-Man or a cake).

Instruction: "Find the cake with the piece missing. Is the missing piece at the top, bottom, left, or right?",.

Measurement: Continue until the patient cannot identify the orientation of the missing sector.

4. Interpretation of Results

Normal: A result of 40 to 60 seconds of arc (often achieving circle #9 on Titmus or similar) is considered normal,,.

Reduced: Performance worse than 60 seconds of arc suggests binocular dysfunction.

Absent: Inability to see the gross target (The Fly) indicates a complete lack of stereopsis, often due to constant strabismus (squint) or severe amblyopia,.

Monocular Cues: Be aware that on the Titmus test, patients can sometimes "guess" the correct circle by looking for the one that looks slightly shifted off-center, even without true 3D vision. The TNO or Randot forms prevent this,.

5. Method to Document (Recording)

Record the specific test used, whether correction was worn, and the level of stereo acuity achieved (in seconds of arc).

Format: [Test Name] [sc/cc] [Result] @ [Distance]

sc = sans correction (without glasses)

cc = cum correction (with glasses)

Gross Stereopsis: If only the fly was seen, record "Gross Stereopsis" or "Fly only",.

Fine Stereopsis: Record the value in seconds of arc (") corresponding to the last correctly identified target (refer to the key on the test booklet),.

Example Recordings:

Normal: Stereo (Titmus): 40" @ 40cm cc.

Reduced: Stereo (Randot): 100" @ 40cm cc.

Poor/Gross only: Stereo (Titmus): Gross only (Fly positive, Animals/Rings negative) @ 40cm.

Absent: Stereo: (-) / Nil @ 40cm.

Clinical Tip Always ensure proper patient positioning, steady fixation target alignment, and clear patient communication before initiating this procedure.
