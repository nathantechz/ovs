Assessment of Tear Production (Schirmer’s Test)

Step-by-step clinical examination protocol, patient instructions, diagnostic interpretation, and practical optometry testing pearls.

1. Purpose of the Test

Evaluate Tear Production: The primary goal is to measure the rate of tear formation and evaluate the integrity of the lacrimal secretory system.

Diagnose Dry Eye: It is the standard clinical test to assess aqueous tear deficiency. It helps differentiate between evaporative dry eye and tear-deficient dry eye (such as Sjögren’s syndrome),.

Assess Total vs. Basic Secretion:

Schirmer Test I (Without Anesthetic): Measures total tear secretion, which includes both basic secretion and reflex tearing caused by the irritation of the paper strip,.

Basic Secretion Test (With Anesthetic): Measures only basic tear secretion by eliminating the reflex tearing component using a topical anesthetic,.

2. Equipment Required

Schirmer Tear Test Strips: Sterile, standardized filter paper strips (5 x 35 mm) packaged in pairs,.

Millimeter Ruler: To measure the wet portion (often printed on the box or included in the strip packaging),.

Tissue: To blot excess tears or dry the eyelids,.

Topical Anesthetic (Optional): Required only if performing the Basic Secretion Test,.

Timer/Watch: To time the 5-minute duration,.

3. Detailed Procedure

Preparation

Patient Comfort: Seat the patient comfortably with their head resting against the headrest. Dimming the room lights can help reduce patient discomfort and reflex tearing due to light sensitivity,.

Strip Preparation: Before opening the sterile package, bend the rounded (wick) end of the strips at the pre-marked notch to approximately a 120° angle. Open the package carefully, handling the strips only by the non-wick (square) end to maintain sterility,.

Lid Preparation: Gently dry the outside of the closed eyelids with a tissue to prevent skin oils or sweat from interfering with the test.

Insertion

Positioning: Instruct the patient to look up. Gently pull the lower eyelid down and temporally,.

Placement: Hook the bent end of the strip over the lower lid margin. Place it at the junction of the temporal (outer) one-third and central one-third of the lower eyelid,.

Critical Step: Ensure the strip touches the palpebral conjunctiva (inside the lid) but does not touch the cornea, as this will cause pain and excessive reflex tearing,.

Duration: Release the eyelid. Instruct the patient to gently close their eyes for 5 minutes.

Note: Closing the eyes is generally preferred to prevent blinking, which acts as a pump, though some protocols allow eyes to remain open and blinking normally,,.

Patient Instruction: Tell the patient to relax, breathe normally, and not to squeeze their eyes shut,.

Measurement

Removal: After 5 minutes, gently remove the strips.

Reading: Measure the length of the wetted area from the notch (bend) downwards in millimeters,.

4. Interpretation of Results

Normal: Greater than 10 mm of wetting in 5 minutes is generally considered normal,,.

Borderline: Between 5 mm and 10 mm.

Dry Eye (Tear Deficiency): Less than 5 mm of wetting indicates a significant aqueous tear deficiency,.

Note: If the result is 0 mm, it indicates a severe deficiency. If the entire strip is wet before 5 minutes, it indicates excessive reflex tearing,.

5. Documentation (Recording Method)

Record the findings for each eye separately (OD for Right, OS for Left).

Test Type: Specify whether it was Schirmer I (no anesthetic) or Basic Secretion (with anesthetic),.

Measurement: Record the amount of wetting in millimeters (mm).

Duration: Confirm the time duration (usually 5 minutes).

Example Recording:

Schirmer I: OD 12mm / OS 8mm (5 mins).

Schirmer (with anesthetic): OD 6mm / OS 5mm.

6. Patient Communication (What to tell the patient)

"This test measures your tear production. It might feel a little scratchy, like an eyelash is in your eye, but it will not be painful.".

"Please keep your eyes gently closed and try to relax for 5 minutes. I will remove the paper strips when the time is up.".

Clinical Tip Always ensure proper patient positioning, steady fixation target alignment, and clear patient communication before initiating this procedure.
