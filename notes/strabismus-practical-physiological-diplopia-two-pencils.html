Physiological Diplopia (Two Pencils)

Step-by-step clinical examination protocol, patient instructions, diagnostic interpretation, and practical optometry testing pearls.

Equipment Needed: Two pencils, pens, or fairly large and obvious objects.

I. Purpose and Principles

Clinical Value: This is a completely free, naturalistic "free-space" method used to check for a fairly gross degree of suppression (deep suppression).

Physiological Basis: When a patient fixates on an object at a given distance, objects located significantly nearer or further than the horopter (outside of Panum's fusional area) are imaged on non-corresponding retinal points. Because these images fall on non-corresponding points, they are perceived as double, a normal feature of binocular vision known as physiological diplopia.

II. Procedure

Hold one pencil at approximately 20 cm and another pencil further away at 40 cm in the midline.

Instruct the patient to binocularly fixate on the farther pencil.

Ask the patient to observe the nearer pencil while maintaining focus on the far one. The nearer pencil should be seen in crossed physiological diplopia (the right eye perceives the image on the left, and the left eye perceives the image on the right).

Next, ask the patient to change fixation to the nearer pencil.

Ask them to observe the farther pencil. The farther pencil should now be seen in uncrossed physiological diplopia.

III. Clinical Observation & Interpretation

Normal Response: The patient successfully perceives the non-fixated pencil as doubled. In everyday vision, the brain uses physiological binocular suppression (or suspension) to ignore these diplopic images outside of Panum's area to prevent symptoms, but the patient can easily become aware of them during this directed test.

Abnormal Response (Suppression): If the patient has difficulty seeing both diplopic images and only sees one of the non-fixated pencils, it indicates they are suppressing an eye. The absence of physiological diplopia reveals a fairly gross degree of suppression.

IV. Advanced Clinical Pearls & Therapeutic Application

Foundation for Vision Therapy: Teaching a patient to appreciate physiological diplopia is often the crucial first step in free-space vision therapy. Once patients master this principle with simple pencils, they can progress to more advanced free-space stereogram exercises (like the "Three Cats" card).

Suppression Check During Convergence Training: Physiological diplopia is frequently utilized as a built-in suppression control during convergence insufficiency treatments, such as push-up exercises or jump convergence. The patient is instructed to fixate on the near target while simultaneously maintaining awareness of the distal target. If the distal target loses its physiological diplopia (is no longer doubled), suppression is occurring, and the targets must be moved further out until the physiological diplopia is regained.

Clinical Tip Always ensure proper patient positioning, steady fixation target alignment, and clear patient communication before initiating this procedure.
