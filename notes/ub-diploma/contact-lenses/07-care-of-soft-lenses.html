Care of Soft Lenses

What each bottle actually does, the disinfection families and how they differ, and why the care regimen depends on the replacement schedule.

WCO competency mapping Category 1 — Optical Technology Services. Selecting a care system and teaching it is performed directly, and is the single biggest lever a diploma optometrist has on infection risk.

1. Regimen follows replacement schedule

ModalityCare required
Daily disposablesBecause of its single use concept, this lens does not require use of surfactant cleaner, disinfecting solution or weekly enzyme. If needed, in-eye re-wetting drops or sterile saline for rinsing prior to insertion
Regular disposables (weekly or bi-weekly)Multi-purpose solutions given as a complete care system. Lenses can be rinsed with aerosol saline prior to insertion or a lubricating solution used to re-wet. No weekly protein removal is needed
Lenses kept longer than a monthAdd periodic protein removal — see below

IACLE M5, p. 23 and p. 18

Do not sell care a patient does not needIACLE is explicit that daily disposables need no cleaner, no disinfectant and no enzyme, and that regular disposables need no weekly protein removal. Prescribing them anyway costs the patient money and teaches them that your instructions are negotiable.

2. The disinfection families

Chemical disinfection systems vary greatly and a wide variety of types exist. Included in the chemical category are hydrogen peroxide and multi-purpose solutions. Chemical disinfection subdivides into oxidative (hydrogen peroxide and chlorine) and conventional cold chemical. IACLE adds, candidly, that "sometimes, the distinctions can seem confusing and complicated to patient and practitioner alike". IACLE M5, p. 12

  • Oxidative — hydrogen peroxide. Effective and preservative-free, which suits sensitive patients. Requires neutralisation, and an unneutralised lens causes a painful chemical injury.
  • Oxidative — chlorine. The other oxidative route.
  • Conventional cold chemical / multi-purpose. One bottle cleans, rinses, disinfects and stores. Convenience is the point, and compliance is better for it — but the preservatives are the source of the problem below.
  • Thermal. Historically standard; IACLE notes a system for thermal disinfection in a domestic microwave oven had recently been released at the time of writing.

IACLE M5, p. 12

The preservative problem, stated by the textbook"The use of strong disinfectants (many of which are also used in lower concentrations as preservatives) in chemical-based disinfection systems can cause problems for the patient." IACLE M5, p. 12 A patient with chronic low-grade redness and stinging on insertion may be reacting to their solution, not their lens.

3. Protein removal

Protein removers are included in the care systems for soft contact lenses, and some RGP lenses, that are not replaced regularly (>1 month). Not all protein removers are enzyme-based; those that are are usually supplied in tablet form, while chemical-based systems are supplied as ready-to-use liquids. IACLE M5, p. 18

They are effective in loosening tightly bound protein deposits. However, they cannot be expected to remove all proteins. Prior to protein removal, the lenses should be cleaned and rinsed before being placed in the recommended container with the tablet or solution. IACLE M5, p. 18

4. Teaching it

IACLE devotes a practical to patient education (Practical 5.1, Use and Care of Contact Lenses). The points that change outcomes:

  1. Wash and dry hands before handling — the step most often skipped and the one that matters most.
  2. Never rinse lenses or the case in tap water. This is the Acanthamoeba route.
  3. Discard and replace solution every time; never top up a case that still has fluid in it.
  4. Air-dry the case face down after rinsing, and replace it regularly.
  5. Do not swim or shower in lenses without advice.
  6. Remove the lens and seek help for any red, painful or photophobic eye — never 'wait and see'.
One sentence worth rehearsing"If your eye is red, painful or light-sensitive, take the lens out and contact us the same day — do not put it back in." Said at every fitting and every aftercare, that sentence prevents more sight loss than any solution you can sell.
