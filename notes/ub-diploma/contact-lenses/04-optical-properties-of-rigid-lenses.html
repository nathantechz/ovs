Optical Properties of Rigid Lenses

The tear lens — the single idea that makes rigid lens power make sense. Why fitting flatter or steeper than K changes the power you must order, and what decentration does to it.

WCO competency mapping Category 1 — Optical Technology Services. Calculating the ordered power from a trial lens and over-refraction is a core dispensing competency.

1. The tear lens exists only under a rigid lens

When a flexible lens is placed on the cornea, the 'tear lens' under the contact lens is very thin. It has no dioptric power due to the conformity of the lens to the shape of the cornea. IACLE M2, p. 135

If a rigid lens is used, the 'tear lens' depends on the relationship between the curvatures of the lens back surface and the cornea and, to a lesser extent, the material's rigidity. IACLE M2, p. 135

This single difference drives everythingA soft lens drapes, so what you order is essentially the ocular refraction adjusted for vertex distance. A rigid lens does not drape, so it creates a liquid lens between itself and the cornea — and that liquid lens has power you must account for.

2. The three fitting relationships

IACLE names the three simplest RGP/cornea relationships as flatter or apical touch, alignment or parallel, and steeper or apical clearance. IACLE M2, p. 135

Flatter than K (apical touch)Minus meniscusMinusMore plus in the lens

SAM–FAPSteeper Add Minus, Flatter Add Plus. The mnemonic is only worth using if you can also say why: a steep fit creates a plus tear lens, so the contact lens must carry more minus for the total to stay correct.

3. Decentration induces prism

If a rigid lens decentres, the tear lens will acquire a prismatic component in addition to the spherical or sphero-cylindrical optics dictated by the fitting relationship. IACLE M2, p. 135

When a rigid lens decentres, and is possibly tilted by upper or lower lid pressures, a prismatic tear lens may be induced under it. In higher powered lenses, any induced tear prismatic effect may be insignificant when compared with the prism induced by the decentred optics. IACLE M2, p. 135

A patient who sees well only sometimesIntermittent blur or doubling in a rigid lens wearer, worse after blinking, points at a lens moving off centre. Two prismatic effects are in play — the tear lens and the decentred lens optics — and in high powers the second dominates. Assess the fit, not the prescription.

4. Over-refraction is how you resolve it

IACLE places Over-Refraction immediately after the tear lens section, and there is a practical exercise devoted to it (Practical 2.3, Contact Lens Over-Refraction). IACLE M2, p. 104

The logic in order:

  1. Place a trial lens of known BOZR and BVP on the eye and let it settle.
  2. Assess the fitting relationship — flat, aligned or steep — since this tells you what the tear lens is doing.
  3. Perform an over-refraction through the trial lens.
  4. Ordered BVP = trial lens BVP + over-refraction, adjusted if the final lens BOZR differs from the trial lens, because changing BOZR changes the tear lens.
The reason over-refraction beats calculationOver-refraction measures the eye with the tear lens already in place. It absorbs the tear lens, vertex distance and any flexure into one reading. Calculate only when you must change the BOZR between trial and final lens.
