Rigid Gas Permeable Lenses: Materials, Manufacturing, Care

What Dk and Dk/t actually mean, how they are measured, what the high-Dk materials cost you in handling, and why RGP care is a different problem from soft lens care.

WCO competency mapping Category 1 — Optical Technology Services. Material selection, verification and care instruction are performed directly at diploma level.

1. Oxygen permeability and transmissibility

These two terms are constantly confused and the distinction is simple once stated. Dk is a property of the material. Dk/t is a property of the lens. IACLE M2, p. 41

Dk/t = Material Dk ÷ tt may be tc (geometric centre thickness) or tLocal, depending on the transmissibility being calculated
  • D = diffusion coefficient of the material
  • k = solubility of the gas in the material
  • Dk is therefore not a function of lens thickness, shape or back vertex power
  • Dk/t divides that permeability by the thickness, so it describes what a particular lens delivers

IACLE M2, p. 41

Why a high-Dk material can still starve a corneaOrder a high-Dk material in a thick, high-minus design and you have diluted the very property you paid for. When a patient shows hypoxic signs, ask about lens thickness and power, not just the material name.

Transmissibility is measured instrumentally. The coulometric technique mounts the lens in an environment-controlled cell and feeds data to a recorder or data logger; Dk is determined indirectly from Dk/t and thickness measurements. The polarographic technique is the main alternative. IACLE M2, p. 41

2. What high-Dk materials cost you

IACLE is candid about the manufacturing disadvantages of fluorosilicone acrylates and silicone acrylates:

  • They are more susceptible to solvent damage during manufacture, and solvents can affect the surface.
  • The back optic zone radius of finished lenses has been known to change over time, especially in high minus back vertex powers.
  • The more exotic materials of high Dk are often difficult to modify, especially in contact lens practice.
  • Reproducibility of lenses fabricated in these materials is lower than that of less permeable materials.

IACLE M2, p. 61

Two practical consequencesFirst, re-verify high-Dk RGPs at aftercare — the BOZR may not be what you ordered, particularly in high minus. Second, do not promise in-practice modification of an exotic high-Dk lens; the textbook says it is often not feasible.

3. Care of rigid lenses

Rigid lenses are not replaced frequently, which changes the care problem. IACLE notes that protein removers are included in the care systems for soft lenses and some RGP lenses, that are not replaced regularly (>1 month). IACLE M5, p. 18

The regimen has distinct steps:

  • Surface cleaning — removes surface debris and loosely bound deposit at the end of each wearing period.
  • Rinsing — removes the cleaner itself before the lens goes near the eye.
  • Disinfection — kills micro-organisms during storage.
  • Protein removal — periodic, for lenses kept longer than a month. Not all protein removers are enzyme-based; those that are are usually supplied in tablet form, chemical-based systems as ready-to-use liquids. They are effective at loosening tightly bound protein deposits, but cannot be expected to remove all proteins.

IACLE M5, p. 18

The step patients skipIACLE is explicit that lenses should be cleaned and rinsed before being placed in the container with the tablet or solution. Patients routinely drop the tablet in with a dirty lens and wonder why deposits persist. Say the order out loud when you teach it: clean, rinse, then protein-remove.

Check yourself

  • Define Dk and Dk/t, and say which is a property of the lens rather than the material.
  • What do D and k stand for?
  • Why is Dk independent of back vertex power but Dk/t is not?
  • Name the two techniques for measuring oxygen transmissibility.
  • Give three manufacturing disadvantages of high-Dk materials.
  • Which lenses need periodic protein removal, and above what replacement interval?
  • What must be done to a lens before protein removal, and why?
