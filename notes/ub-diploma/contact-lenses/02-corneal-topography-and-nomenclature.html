Corneal Topography and Contact Lens Nomenclature

The vocabulary and symbols used on every lens order and verification form — and the corneal measurements those numbers have to match.

WCO competency mapping Category 1 — Optical Technology Services. Specifying and verifying a lens correctly is the foundation of everything else in this course.

1. The parameters, and their symbols

Every lens is described by the same small set of parameters. IACLE's symbols are the ones used on order forms and in the literature, and are worth learning exactly.

SymbolParameter
r0Back Optic Zone Radius (BOZR)
Ø0Back Optic Zone Diameter (BOZD)
Øa0Front Optic Zone Diameter (FOZD)
Ø1Back Peripheral Zone Diameter (BPZD)
ØTTotal Diameter (TD)
tcGeometric centre thickness
tEA / tERAxial and radial edge thickness
Fv′Back Vertex Power (BVP)
FvFront Vertex Power (FVP)

IACLE M2, p. 162

BVP, not FVPA contact lens is specified and verified by its back vertex power, because that is the surface nearest the eye and therefore what determines the correction. A lensmeter reading taken with the lens the wrong way round gives FVP, and for a high-powered lens the two differ enough to matter.

2. Design factors that change on-eye performance

IACLE lists the soft lens design factors, noting that each can affect 'on-eye' performance:

  • Geometric centre thickness (tc)
  • Lens diameter (total diameter, TD, ØT)
  • Back optic zone radius (BOZR, r0)
  • Back surface design

IACLE M2, p. 162

Thickness is not only about comfortCentre thickness sets oxygen transmissibility, because Dk/t divides the material's permeability by the thickness. A thicker lens in the same material delivers less oxygen. Thickness is a physiological parameter, not just a handling one.

3. Corneal measurement

The lens has to match a cornea, so the cornea has to be measured. IACLE's optics lecture devotes a section to Corneal Radius of Curvature, sitting immediately before The Tear Lens and Over-Refraction — the order tells you how the three connect. IACLE M2, p. 104

Two approaches, with different reach:

  • Keratometry measures the radius of curvature of a small central zone, typically about 3 mm, in two principal meridians. Quick, reproducible, and the basis of first trial lens selection.
  • Corneal topography maps thousands of points across the whole cornea, showing peripheral flattening, irregularity and asymmetry that keratometry averages away.
Why keratometry alone was rejected in 1946A 1946 patent application for a corneal lens was refused, one ground being that "many eyes present corneal surfaces of irregular curvature; keratometer measurements alone would not avail." IACLE M2, p. 19 That objection is exactly why topography exists. Keratometry gives you a starting lens; it does not describe the cornea.

Check yourself

  • Write the symbols for back optic zone radius, total diameter and back vertex power.
  • Why is a contact lens specified by BVP rather than FVP?
  • List the four soft lens design factors IACLE names.
  • Explain why centre thickness is a physiological parameter.
  • What does keratometry measure, and over roughly what zone?
  • Give the 1946 objection to relying on keratometry, and say what modern instrument answers it.
