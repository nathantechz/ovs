Eyelid Malpositions: Entropion, Ectropion and Dermatitis

Why an ageing lid turns in or out, the bedside tests that show which mechanism has failed, and why entropion threatens the cornea while ectropion threatens the tear film.

WCO competency mapping Category 3 — Ocular Diagnostic Services. Recognition, the lid laxity tests, and correct referral. Temporary protective measures such as lubricants and taping fall within diploma scope.

1. The two malpositions

One sentence that separates themEntropion is a corneal problem — inturned lashes abrade the epithelium. Ectropion is a tear film and surface problem — the punctum no longer reaches the tear lake, so the eye waters while the exposed conjunctiva dries.

2. Involutional ectropion

Involutional (age-related) ectropion affects the lower lid of elderly patients. It causes epiphora (tear overflow) and may exacerbate ocular surface disease. The red appearance of the exposed conjunctiva is cosmetically poor. In long-standing cases the tarsal conjunctiva may become chronically inflamed, thickened and keratinized. Kanski 8e, p. 45

The horizontal lid laxity test

This is the key bedside test and it has a precise threshold:

  1. Take the central part of the lower lid between finger and thumb.
  2. Pull it away from the globe. Laxity is demonstrated by pulling the lid 8 mm or more from the globe.
  3. Release it and watch. The abnormal finding is failure to snap back to its normal position without the patient first blinking.

Kanski 8e, p. 45

The detail candidates lose marks onIt is not enough that the lid returns slowly. The test is positive if it needs a blink to reseat. Watch the lid, not the clock, and do not let the patient blink until you have seen the result.

3. Other types of ectropion

  • Paralytic ectropion — from facial nerve palsy. Permanent treatment options include medial canthoplasty and a lateral canthal sling, in which a refashioned canthal tendon from the lower lid is passed through a buttonhole in the tendon from the upper lid.
  • Mechanical ectropion — caused by tumours on or near the lid margin dragging it outward.
  • Cicatricial ectropion — scarring of the skin shortening the anterior lamella.

Kanski 8e, pp. 45–50, Fig. 1.66

Mechanical ectropion is a referral, urgentlyA lid pulled out of position by a mass means there is a mass. Do not record "ectropion" and arrange a review — examine the lid margin properly and refer any lesion.

4. Involutional entropion — four mechanisms

Kanski lists the contributing factors. Each is testable at the slit lamp:

  • Horizontal lid laxity — the same test as for ectropion.
  • Vertical lid instability caused by attenuation, dehiscence or disinsertion of the lower lid retractors. Weakness is recognised by decreased excursion of the lower lid in downgaze.
  • Over-riding of the pretarsal by the preseptal orbicularis during lid closure. This moves the lower border of the tarsal plate anteriorly, away from the globe, and the upper border towards the globe — tipping the lid inwards.
  • Orbital septum laxity with prolapse of orbital fat into the lower lid.

Kanski 8e, p. 50, Fig. 1.67B

Test the retractors in ten secondsAsk the patient to look down and watch the lower lid. A normal lid follows the eye downward. A lid that stays put has weak retractors — the second mechanism on the list, and one you can document without any equipment.

5. Management within your scope

For entropion, temporary protection must be as short-term as possible. Options include lubricants, taping, soft bandage contact lenses and orbicularis chemodenervation with botulinum toxin. Kanski 8e, p. 50

Read that instruction carefully"As short-term as possible" is the textbook telling you that taping is a bridge to surgery, not a management plan. Every day of inturned lashes is another day of corneal abrasion. Tape the lid and make the referral.

6. Eyelid dermatitis

Eyelid skin is the thinnest in the body and reacts readily. Kanski associates specific skin disease with specific forms of lid margin disease, which is the practical link for this topic.

Lid conditionCommonly associated skin disease
Staphylococcal anterior blepharitisAtopic dermatitis
Seborrhoeic anterior blepharitisSeborrhoeic dermatitis
Posterior blepharitis (MGD)Acne rosacea

Kanski 8e, Table 1.4, p. 34

Angular blepharitis is usually caused by Moraxella lacunata or S. aureus. Red, scaly, macerated and fissured skin is seen at the lateral and/or medial canthi. Note that skin chafing secondary to tear overflow, especially at the lateral canthus, can cause a similar clinical picture and may predispose to infection. Treatment involves topical chloramphenicol, bacitracin or erythromycin. Kanski 8e, p. 38

The loop you must spotEctropion causes tear overflow. Tear overflow chafes the lateral canthal skin. Chafed skin looks like — and predisposes to — angular blepharitis. Treating the infection without correcting the lid position guarantees recurrence.
