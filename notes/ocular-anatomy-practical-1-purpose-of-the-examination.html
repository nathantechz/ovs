Ocular Anatomy & Physiology · Clinical Skills

1. Purpose of the Examination

Step-by-step clinical examination protocol, patient instructions, diagnostic interpretation, and practical optometry testing pearls.

The goals of this examination are to:

Identify Abnormalities: Detect gross anatomical irregularities such as ptosis (drooping of the upper lid), ectropion (outward turning of the lower lid), entropion (inward turning of the lid), or trichiasis (aberrant growth of lashes rubbing against the eye),.

Assess Health: Evaluate the lid margins for signs of inflammation or infection, such as blepharitis(crusting/redness) or meibomian gland dysfunction.

Establish Baselines: Obtain measurements of the palpebral fissure (aperture) and lid position, which are critical for contact lens fitting and monitoring neurological health (e.g., Cranial Nerve III or VII function),,.

2. Equipment Required

Penlight or Transilluminator: For gross observation and evaluating pupillary reflexes,.

Slit Lamp Biomicroscope: For detailed magnified inspection of the lid margins and lashes.

Millimeter Ruler (PD Ruler): To measure the palpebral fissure width and pupil size,.

3. Detailed Procedures

Part A: Gross Inspection (Penlight Examination)

Activity: Inspect eyelids for position and orientation.

General Observation: Sit facing the patient at eye level. Using a penlight or room light, observe the patient's face for symmetry, head tilts, or unusual posture.

Inspect Eyelids:

Look for ptosis (drooping) or asymmetry between the left and right eyes,.

Check lid apposition: Ensure the lids close completely during a blink (lagophthalmos) and that the lower lid is not turned outward (ectropion) or inward (entropion),.

Inspect Lashes:

Observe the direction of the lashes. Note if any lashes are growing inward toward the cornea (trichiasis).

Check for missing lashes or misdirected growth.

Part B: Slit Lamp Examination

Activity: Inspect lid margins for inflammation (blepharitis).

Setup: Set the slit lamp magnification to low ($6\times$ or $10\times$) and use a diffuse beam or wide parallelepiped. Angle the illumination arm approximately $30^{\circ}$ from the straight-ahead position.

Scan Upper Lid: Ask the patient to close their eyes. Scan across the skin of the upper eyelid and lashes from the temporal to the nasal canthus. Look for redness, crusting, or scales at the base of the lashes (signs of blepharitis),.

Scan Lower Lid & Margins:

Ask the patient to look up. Scan the lower lid margin and lashes.

Examine the meibomian gland orifices on the lid margin for capping (blocked glands) or secretions (frothing or thickened oil),.

Check the position of the punctum (tear drain) to ensure it is open and against the globe.

Part C: Measurements

Measurement: Palpebral fissure width and levator function.

Palpebral Aperture (Fissure) Size:

Position: Instruct the patient to look at a distant target. Discourage them from staring wide-eyed or squinting.

Measure: Place a millimeter ruler vertically in front of the patient's eye (avoiding contact with lashes). Measure the maximum vertical distance between the upper and lower eyelid margins at the widest point,.

Normal Findings: The typical aperture size ranges from 8 to 11 mm.

Lid Position (Levator Function Assessment):

Primary Gaze: Observe where the upper and lower lids cross the cornea. A normal upper lid typically covers the top one-third of the cornea (or is tangent to the limbus), while the lower lid rests at or just below the inferior limbus.

Upward Gaze (Levator Check): To screen for ptosis or levator dysfunction, measure the aperture size in straight-ahead gaze. Then, instruct the patient to follow a target into upward gaze. Observe if the upper lid clears the limbus and compare the movement of both eyes. If one lid clears the limbus later than the other, it indicates a ptosis,.

4. Documentation (Recording Method)

Record findings for each eye (OD for right, OS for left) separately.

Gross/Slit Lamp Findings:

Normal: Record "WNL" (Within Normal Limits) or "Clear" if no pathology is found.

Abnormal: Describe the condition and severity (e.g., "Grade 1 blepharitis," "Trichiasis temporal lower lid," "Ectropion medial").

Example:

Lids/Lashes: Clear, no crusting.

Margins: Meibomian glands capped OD.

Measurements:

Aperture Size: Record the vertical height in millimeters.

Lid Position: You may draw a simple diagram showing the lid crossing the cornea or describe it (e.g., "Upper lid at 10:00 and 2:00").

Example:

Palpebral Aperture: 10 mm OD / 9 mm OS.

Ptosis: (-) Negative (or "No ptosis"),,.

Clinical Tip Always ensure proper patient positioning, steady fixation target alignment, and clear patient communication before initiating this procedure.
