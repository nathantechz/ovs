Part A: Amsler Grid Test

Step-by-step clinical examination protocol, patient instructions, diagnostic interpretation, and practical optometry testing pearls.

1. Purpose

Evaluate Macular Integrity: To test the central 10 to 20 degrees of the visual field for dysfunctions.

Detect Metamorphopsia: To identify macular distortion (wavy lines), which is a common symptom of macular edema or age-related macular degeneration (ARMD).

Detect Scotomas: To identify central or paracentral blind spots.

2. Equipment Required

Amsler Grid Chart: Typically "Chart 1," which is a white grid on a black background with a central fixation dot. Each square measures 5 mm.

Occluder: To test each eye individually.

Reading Correction: The patient’s reading glasses or trial lenses for near vision.

Good Lighting: To ensure high visibility of the grid.

3. Detailed Procedure

Preparation: Seat the patient comfortably. Ensure they are wearing their near correction (reading glasses or bifocals) because the test is performed at a near distance.

Distance: Hold the chart (or have the patient hold it) at a distance of 30 cm (approximately 12–14 inches) from the eye.

Monocular Testing: Instruct the patient to cover their left eye to test the right eye first.

Fixation: Ask the patient to look directly at the central white dot and keep their focus there throughout the entire test.

The "Questions" (Assessment): While the patient maintains fixation on the center dot, ask the following:

"Can you see the white dot in the center?" (Checks for a central scotoma).

"Can you see all four corners and sides of the big square?" (Checks for larger defects like glaucoma).

"Are any of the small squares missing or blurry?" (Checks for paracentral scotomas).

"Do any of the lines look wavy, bent, or distorted?" (Checks for metamorphopsia).

Repetition: Repeat the procedure for the left eye.

4. Documentation (Recording)

Normal: If no defects are seen, record "WNL" (Within Normal Limits) or "Negative" for each eye.

Example: Amsler: OD WNL / OS WNL.

Abnormal: If the patient reports defects, give them a recording sheet (a paper copy of the grid) and ask them to draw the area of distortion or blindness. Describe the location (e.g., "superior-temporal distortion").

Example: Amsler: OD lines wavy centrally; OS WNL.

Part B: Color Vision Test (Ishihara Plates)

1. Purpose

Screen for Congenital Defects: To detect hereditary red-green color deficiencies (common in males), which are usually symmetrical in both eyes.

Screen for Acquired Defects: To detect pathology of the optic nerve or macula (e.g., optic neuritis or macular edema), which often presents as asymmetric or monocular color loss.

2. Equipment Required

Pseudoisochromatic Plates Booklet: The Ishihara test is the standard for red-green screening.

Correct Illumination: "Daylight" illumination (e.g., a Macbeth lamp or a daylight LED/fluorescent bulb) is critical, as standard tungsten bulbs can alter color perception.

Occluder: For monocular testing.

3. Detailed Procedure

Preparation: The patient should wear their near correction (reading glasses). Avoid tinted glasses.

Distance: Hold the booklet approximately 75 cm from the patient, tilted so the light falls clearly on the page.

Monocular Testing: For a general screening, binocular testing is sometimes done, but to assess macular or optic nerve function (pathology), you must test each eye separately (monocularly) to detect asymmetry.

Demonstration: Show the first plate (usually a number "12"). Everyone, including those with color blindness, should see this. This confirms the patient understands the task and is not malingering.

Testing: Flip through the plates, allowing the patient about 3 seconds per plate to identify the number seen.

Tracing (Illiterate/Children): If the patient cannot read numbers, use the plates with winding colored lines and ask them to trace the path with a brush or finger.

4. Interpretation

Normal: The patient reads the numbers correctly (e.g., 15 out of 16 plates).

Red-Green Deficiency: The patient misses specific plates or sees a different number than a normal observer (transformation plates).

Acquired Defect: If the patient makes errors in one eye but not the other, or if they report the colors look "washed out" (desaturated) in one eye, suspect optic nerve or macular disease.

5. Documentation (Recording)

Record the fraction of plates identified correctly out of the total tested for each eye.

Note if the test was monocular or binocular.

Example (Normal): Color (Ishihara): 14/14 OD, 14/14 OS.

Example (Defect): Color (Ishihara): 8/14 OD (Deutan pattern), 14/14 OS. (Suggests acquired defect due to asymmetry).

Clinical Tip Always ensure proper patient positioning, steady fixation target alignment, and clear patient communication before initiating this procedure.
