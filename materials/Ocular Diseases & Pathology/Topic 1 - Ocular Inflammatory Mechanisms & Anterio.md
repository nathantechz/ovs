# Ocular Diseases & Pathology - Topic 1: Ocular Inflammatory Mechanisms & Anterior Uveitis
## Bachelor of Optometry &middot; Ocular Diseases Curriculum

---

## Overview & Objectives
Breakdown of the blood-aqueous barrier, keratic precipitates (KPs), flare vs. cells, iris nodules (Koeppe, Busacca), and posterior synechiae.

## Core Concepts & Mechanisms

### SUN Classification of Anterior Chamber Cells
Grade 0 (<1 cell), 0.5+ (1-5 cells), 1+ (6-15 cells), 2+ (16-25 cells), 3+ (26-50 cells), 4+ (>50 cells with hypopyon).

### Granulomatous vs. Non-Granulomatous
Granulomatous presents with large 'mutton-fat' KPs (macrophages/epithelioid cells) associated with Sarcoidosis, TB, Syphilis. Non-granulomatous presents with fine KPs (HLA-B27, Ankylosing Spondylitis).

### Complications of Uveitis
Posterior synechiae (seclusio pupillae leading to iris bombé and acute angle closure), band keratopathy, secondary cataract, and cystoid macular edema.

## Clinical Pearls & Practice Links
> **Clinical Link:** Immediate cycloplegia (e.g., Cyclopentolate 1% or Homatropine 5%) is mandatory in active uveitis to prevent posterior synechiae and relieve ciliary spasm pain.
