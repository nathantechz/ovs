# Practical Clinical Guide: Estimation of Anterior Chamber Depth using the Penlight Method (also known as the Shadow Test).
## Ocular Anatomy & Physiology &middot; Clinical Examination Protocol

---

## Clinical Procedure & Protocol


### 1. Purpose of the Test

Safety Precaution: The primary purpose is to estimate the depth of the anterior chamber angle as a safety precaution before instilling mydriatic (dilating) drops,.

Assess Risk of Angle Closure: It helps identify eyes with narrow anterior angles that may be at risk of developing acute angle-closure glaucoma if the pupil is dilated,,.

Screening Tool: It acts as a quick, simple screening technique when a slit lamp biomicroscope is not available (e.g., during domiciliary visits) or when a patient cannot be positioned at a slit lamp (e.g., young children or wheelchair users),.


### 2. Equipment Required


### Penlight (Torch): A focused light source,.


### Dimly Lit Room: To enhance the visibility of the shadow,.


### 3. Detailed Procedure


### Preparation

Lighting: Dim the room lights to improve contrast, making the shadow on the iris easier to see,.

Patient Instruction: Ask the patient to open their eyes and look straight ahead at a distant target,.

Execution 3.  Positioning the Light: Hold the penlight a few centimeters from the patient's temporal (outer) canthus (the corner of the eye),. 4.  Angulation: Hold the light in the horizontal plane of the eye. Direct the beam at an angle of approximately 100° from the temporal side, and then rotate it to 90° (perpendicular) to the visual axis, shining it across the plane of the iris,. *  Concept: You are shining the light across the front of the iris from the ear side toward the nose side. 5.  Observation: Observe the nasal (nose side) half of the iris. Look for a shadow cast by the iris itself,. *  Open Angle (Deep Chamber): If the iris is flat (typical of a deep chamber), the light will pass across the entire iris surface and illuminate the nasal side. No shadow will be seen,. *  Narrow Angle (Shallow Chamber): If the anterior chamber is shallow, the iris is often bowed forward (convex). The temporal part of the bowed iris blocks the light, casting a crescent-shaped shadow on the nasal side of the iris,.


### Repetition 6. Repeat the procedure for the left eye,.


### 4. Interpretation (Grading System)

The depth of the angle is estimated based on how much of the nasal iris is covered by shadow. The wider the shadow, the narrower the angle and the higher the risk of closure.

Grade IV (Deep/Open): 0% of the nasal iris is in shadow. The entire iris is illuminated.

Risk: Impossible to close,.


### Grade III: 25% of the nasal iris is in shadow.

Risk: Unlikely to close (Safe to dilate),.


### Grade II: 50% of the nasal iris is in shadow.

Risk: Angle closure is possible. Gonioscopy is recommended before dilation,.


### Grade I: 75% of the nasal iris is in shadow.

Risk: Angle closure is very likely. Dilation is generally contraindicated without further assessment,.


### Grade 0 (Closed): 100% of the nasal iris is in shadow.

Risk: Angle is closed (100% risk),.


### 5. Method to Document (Recording)

Record the test name ("Shadow Test" or "Penlight Angle") and the estimated Grade or the percentage of iris in shadow.


### Example 1 (Normal):

Shadow Test: Grade IV OU (Open).

Or: Shadow test: <10% shadow OU.


### Example 2 (Narrow):

Shadow Test: Grade II OD (50% shadow), Grade I OS (75% shadow).

Note: Significant shadow noted; Gonioscopy indicated before dilation.


### Example 3 (Descriptive):

Angles: Deep/Open to penlight OU.
