# Practical Clinical Guide: Assessment of Corneal Sensitivity
## Ocular Anatomy & Physiology &middot; Clinical Examination Protocol

---

## Clinical Procedure & Protocol


### 1. Purpose of the Test

Evaluate Nerve Function: To test the functional integrity of the Trigeminal Nerve (CN V), specifically the ophthalmic division, which provides sensation (touch/pain) to the cornea.

Assess Protective Reflex: To verify the presence of the corneal reflex (blinking), which protects the eye from injury.

Detect Asymmetry: To compare the sensation between the two eyes to detect unilateral problems (e.g., nerve palsy, herpes simplex keratitis, or neurotrophic keratopathy).

Important Pre-requisite: This test must be performed before any anesthetic drops are instilled in the eye, as anesthetics will numb the cornea and invalidate the test.


### 2. Equipment Required

Cotton-Tipped Applicators: Sterile swabs with the cotton pulled out to form a fine wisp or point.

Aesthesiometer (Optional): An instrument (such as the Cochet-Bonnet) that uses fine nylon filaments of varying lengths to quantitatively measure sensitivity.


### 3. Detailed Procedures


### Method A: Cotton Wisp Test (Qualitative)

This is the most common clinical screening method.

Preparation: Take a sterile cotton-tipped applicator and gently pull a small amount of cotton from the tip to form a fine, thin wisp or point. Prepare one for each eye to maintain sterility.

Patient Instruction: Ask the patient to open their eyes wide. Instruct them to look up and away from the side being tested (e.g., look up and to the left when testing the right eye).

Approach: Approach the patient's eye from the side (out of their direct line of sight) so they do not blink simply because they see your hand moving.

Contact: Gently touch the cornea (not the white sclera) near the limbus (the edge of the colored part) with the fine cotton wisp.

Precaution: Be very careful not to touch the eyelashes or the eyelid margins, as this will trigger a different blink reflex and give a false positive result.

Observation: Observe the immediate reaction. A normal response is a brisk reflex blink of the eyelids.

Comparison: Repeat the procedure on the fellow eye using a fresh wisp. Compare the strength and speed of the blink response between the right and left eyes.


### Method B: Aesthesiometer (Quantitative)

This method is used when a specific measurement is required.

Setup: The instrument uses a fine nylon filament that can be adjusted in length.


### Contact: Gently touch the cornea with the filament.

Measurement: Shorten the filament length until the patient feels the sensation or blinks. The length of the filament required to elicit a response corresponds to the corneal sensitivity level.


### 4. Documentation (Recording Method)

Record the findings for each eye or compare them.

Normal: If the blink reflex is equal and brisk in both eyes, record:


### "Trigeminal nerves intact".


### "Corneal sensitivity normal/symmetrical OU".


### "Corneal reflex: +/+" (indicating present in both).

Abnormal: If sensation is reduced or absent in one eye, describe the asymmetry:


### "Reduced corneal sensitivity OD".


### "Diminished blink reflex OS".


### "Corneal reflex: Absent OD / Present OS".

Aesthesiometer: Record the length of the filament (in mm) that elicited a response (e.g., "55mm OD / 30mm OS").
