# Practical Clinical Guide: Evaluation of Extraocular Muscles (Range of Motion) using the "H" Pattern Test.
## Ocular Anatomy & Physiology &middot; Clinical Examination Protocol

---

## Clinical Procedure & Protocol


### 1. Purpose of the Test

Assess Range of Motion: To evaluate the ability of the eyes to perform conjugate movements (versions) and move into all diagnostic positions of gaze,.

Detect Incomitancy: To identify if the angle of deviation changes in different gaze positions, which suggests a muscle paresis (weakness) or mechanical restriction,.

Identify Muscle Dysfunction: To check for the under-action or over-action of specific extraocular muscles,.

Symptom Check: To determine if specific eye movements cause diplopia (double vision) or pain/discomfort,.


### 2. Equipment Required

Penlight: A focused light source is preferred because it creates corneal reflexes that help the examiner detect subtle misalignments,.

Target (Optional): A non-luminous target (like a fixation stick) can be used, but a penlight is generally better for observing reflexes.


### 3. Detailed Procedure (The "H" Pattern)


### Preparation

Lighting: Keep room lights on to illuminate the patient's eyes without shadows,.

Spectacles: Ask the patient to remove their glasses. Frames can hide the eyes in extreme gaze, and prismatic effects from high-power lenses can induce artificial diplopia,.

Positioning: Sit directly in front of the patient at eye level.


### Patient Instructions

Head Still: Instruct the patient to follow the light with their eyes only and not to move their head,,.

Reporting: Tell the patient: "Tell me if you see the light become double or if you feel any pain or pulling sensation.",.


### Execution (The Broad "H")

Primary Position: Start with the light directly in front of the patient at a distance of 40 cm,.

The Arc: Move the penlight in a broad "H" pattern. It is critical to move the light in an arc (keeping the distance from the nose constant) rather than a flat plane, so the accommodation demand doesn't change,.


### The Six Cardinal Positions:


### Step 1 (Right): Move the light to the patient’s right.

Muscles tested: Right Lateral Rectus (RLR) & Left Medial Rectus (LMR),.


### Step 2 (Up & Right): From the right, move the light up.

Muscles tested: Right Superior Rectus (RSR) & Left Inferior Oblique (LIO),.

Step 3 (Down & Right): Move the light back down through the midline to the down-right position.

Muscles tested: Right Inferior Rectus (RIR) & Left Superior Oblique (LSO),.

Step 4 (Left): Move the light across the center to the patient's left.

Muscles tested: Left Lateral Rectus (LLR) & Right Medial Rectus (RMR),.


### Step 5 (Up & Left): From the left, move the light up.

Muscles tested: Left Superior Rectus (LSR) & Right Inferior Oblique (RIO),.

Step 6 (Down & Left): Move the light down to the down-left position.

Muscles tested: Left Inferior Rectus (LIR) & Right Superior Oblique (RSO),.

Note: You may need to use your free hand to gently lift the patient's upper eyelid during downward gaze to clearly see the eye,.


### Observation Points

Symmetry: Watch the corneal light reflexes. If they remain centered (or symmetrical), the eyes are aligned. If one reflex shifts, it indicates a deviation,.

Smoothness: The eyes should move smoothly (pursuit movement). Look for jerkiness or nystagmus,.

Note: A small amount of jerking at the very extreme of the gaze (end-point nystagmus) is normal,.

Extent: Ensure both eyes move fully into the corners. If one eye stops short, it is an underaction,.


### 4. Follow-up (If Defect is Found)

Ductions: If one eye seems to lag (underaction) during the binocular test (versions), cover the "good" eye and ask the patient to move the "bad" eye into that position again. This tests ductions (monocular movement).

If the eye moves fully when the other is covered, it suggests a muscle paresis (weakness).

If the eye still cannot move fully, it suggests a mechanical restriction,,.


### 5. Method to Document (Recording)

Normal Findings: Use the acronym SAFE or FESA to record normal motility:


### S: Smooth


### A: Accurate


### F: Full


### E: Extensive (range of motion),,.

Example: "Motility: SAFE, (-) diplopia/pain."


### Abnormal Findings:

Diagram: Draw a cross or an "H" and mark the position where the abnormality occurred,.


### Grading Scale:

Underaction: Record as negative numbers from -1 to -4 (where -4 is maximum weakness/no movement past the midline),.


### Overaction: Record as positive numbers from +1 to +4,.

Diplopia/Pain: Record clearly where the patient saw double or felt pain.

Example: "Diplopia on up-right gaze" or "Pain on left gaze",.


### Example Recording:

Motility: RLR underaction (-2), LMR overaction (+2). Diplopia in right gaze.

Or: SAFE OU. No diplopia.
