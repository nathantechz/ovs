# Practical Clinical Guide: Intraocular Pressure Measurement (Tonometry)
## Ocular Anatomy & Physiology &middot; Clinical Examination Protocol

---

## Clinical Procedure & Protocol


### 1. Purpose of the Test

Diagnose Glaucoma: To detect elevated intraocular pressure (IOP), a primary risk factor for glaucoma.

Monitor Treatment: To assess the effectiveness of glaucoma medications or surgeries in lowering IOP.

Screening: To identify "glaucoma suspects" or ocular hypertensives (high pressure without optic nerve damage).

Normal Range: While the user query specifies 10–21 mm Hg, clinical texts note slight variations, such as 8–21 mm Hg or 10–21 mm Hg. Pressures consistently above 21 mm Hg are generally considered ocular hypertension, though glaucoma can exist at "normal" pressures (low-tension glaucoma).


### 2. Method A: Goldmann Applanation Tonometry (GAT)

This is the "Gold Standard" for IOP measurement. It measures the force required to flatten (applanate) a 3.06 mm diameter circular area of the cornea.


### Equipment Required:

Slit Lamp Biomicroscope with Goldmann Tonometer arm.

Tonometer Prism (disinfected).

Topical Anesthetic (e.g., Proparacaine) and Sodium Fluorescein strips (or combined solution like Fluress).


### Detailed Procedure:

Disinfection: Ensure the tonometer prism has been disinfected (e.g., with a bleach dilution or hydrogen peroxide) and rinsed/dried to prevent infection transmission.


### Patient Preparation:

Instill anesthetic and fluorescein into the lower cul-de-sac of both eyes.

instruct the patient to blink to distribute the dye.

Check the cornea for staining (abrasions) before performing tonometry, as the tonometer can cause artifacts.


### Instrument Setup:

Insert the prism into the holder. Align the white mark on the holder with 0° (or 180°) on the prism.

Note: If the patient has high corneal astigmatism (>3D), align the red mark on the holder with the patient's minus cylinder axis to prevent errors.

Set the magnification to low ($10\times$ or $16\times$).

Insert the Cobalt Blue filter.

Open the slit beam to its widest setting.

Angle the illumination arm 45° to 60° from the microscope.

Preset the pressure dial to 15 mm Hg (1.5 gm) to minimize adjustment time.


### Measurement:

Ask the patient to look straight ahead and keep eyes wide open. You may need to gently hold the lids open, pinning the upper lid against the brow bone (avoid pressing on the eyeball, which artificially raises IOP).

Looking from the side (outside the oculars), move the joystick forward until the prism almost touches the cornea (the limbus will "glow" blue upon contact).

Look through the left ocular (the one aligned with the prism path). You will see two green semi-circles (mires).


### Alignment:

Centering: Adjust the joystick so the two semi-circles are centered and of equal size.

Pressure: Turn the drum (dial) until the inner edges of the two semi-circles just touch (creating a sideways "S" shape).


### Reading the Value:

Pull the prism back from the cornea.

Read the number on the dial and multiply by 10 (e.g., "1.6" = 16 mm Hg).


### Troubleshooting Mires (Fluorescein Width):

Correct: Mires should be about 1/10th the diameter of the circle.

Too Thick: Too much fluorescein or tearing. The reading will be artificially high. Blot the eye with a tissue.

Too Thin: Not enough fluorescein. The reading will be artificially low. Instill more dye.


### 3. Method B: Tono-Pen (Electronic Indentation/Applanation)

A portable, handheld device useful for patients who cannot sit at a slit lamp (e.g., wheelchair, supine) or have irregular corneas.


### Detailed Procedure:

Calibration: Perform a calibration check at the start of the day. Hold the button, wait for "CAL," then invert the pen upside down as instructed until it reads "GOOD".

Preparation: Slide a sterile Ocu-Film (latex cover) over the tip. Instill anesthetic drops in the patient's eyes.


### Measurement:

Instruct the patient to look straight ahead at a distant target.

Hold the Tono-Pen like a pencil.

Press the operator button once to activate (it will beep).

Lightly and repeatedly tap the central cornea with the tip. Do not press hard; a very light touch is sufficient.

The device will click/chirp with each valid reading. After 4–6 valid taps, it will beep again and display the average IOP.

Check the statistical confidence bar (e.g., 5%, 10%, 20%). A lower percentage indicates a more reliable reading.


### 4. Method C: Non-Contact Tonometry (NCT / "Air Puff")

Useful for screening, as it requires no anesthetic.


### Detailed Procedure:

Setup: Explain the "puff of air" to the patient so they are not startled. No anesthetic is needed.

Alignment: Align the patient in the chinrest. Using the joystick, align the central target (often a red dot in a circle) on the patient's cornea.

Measurement: When perfectly aligned, the instrument fires a puff of air automatically (or manually).

Repetition: It is recommended to take 3 to 4 readings per eye and average them to account for the cardiac pulse, which can fluctuate IOP by 4 mm Hg.


### 5. Important Clinical Considerations

Corneal Thickness: GAT assumes an average corneal thickness (approx. 555 microns).


### Thick Corneas: Will give artificially high readings.

Thin Corneas (e.g., post-LASIK): Will give artificially low readings.

Diurnal Variation: IOP fluctuates throughout the day, often peaking in the morning. Variations of 3–4 mm Hg are normal.


### 6. Documentation (Method to Document)

Record the findings clearly in the patient's chart.

Format: The standard notation is a "T" shape or specific labels for Right (OD) and Left (OS).


### Information to Include:


### IOP Value: In mm Hg.

Instrument Used: (e.g., Goldmann/GAT, NCT, Tono-Pen, Perkins).


### Time of Day: Essential due to diurnal variation.

Drops Used: Anesthetic and Dilation status (IOP should be measured before dilation if possible).


### Example 1 (Goldmann):

IOP (GAT): 15 / 16 mm Hg @ 10:30 AM.

Drops: Proparacaine/NaFl.


### Example 2 (NCT):

NCT: OD 18, 19, 18 (Avg 18) / OS 20, 21, 20 (Avg 20) @ 2:00 PM.


### Example 3 (T-Notation):

15 $T_{GAT}$ 16 @ 9:00 AM.
