# Practical Clinical Guide: Evaluation of Tear Film Stability (TBUT)
## Ocular Anatomy & Physiology &middot; Clinical Examination Protocol

---

## Clinical Procedure & Protocol


### 1. Purpose of the Test

Assess Stability: To measure the stability of the precorneal tear film,.

Evaluate Layers: The test assesses the integrity of the mucin and lipid layers. Dry spots form due to the evaporation of the tear film or the migration of superficial lipid through the aqueous layer to the mucin surface,.

Diagnose Dry Eye: A reduced TBUT indicates a dry eye condition, specifically evaporative dry eye or mucin deficiency,.

Contact Lens Baseline: It is essential to measure TBUT prior to fitting contact lenses to determine if the patient is a suitable candidate,.


### 2. Equipment Required

Slit Lamp Biomicroscope: For magnification and illumination,.


### Sodium Fluorescein Strips: To stain the tear film,.


### Sterile Saline Solution: To wet the dye strips,.


### Stopwatch or Watch: To count seconds accurately.

Wratten #12 Yellow Filter (Optional): This enhances the contrast of the fluorescein when used with the cobalt blue filter, making the dry spots easier to see,.


### 3. Detailed Procedure


### Preparation

Anesthetic Check: Do not use a topical anesthetic (like proparacaine) before this test if possible. Anesthetics can alter the composition of the tear film and artificially reduce the TBUT,,.

Strip Preparation: Wet the tip of a fluorescein strip with a drop of sterile saline. Shake off any excess fluid to prevent flooding the eye or staining the patient's cheek,.


### Instillation

Application: Instruct the patient to look up or to the side. Gently pull down the lower eyelid. Touch the moistened strip to the inferior bulbar conjunctiva (the white of the eye) or the temporal cul-de-sac,,.

Precaution: Do not touch the cornea with the strip, as this can cause reflex tearing or abrasions, invalidating the test,.

Distribution: Ask the patient to blink several times. This spreads the dye evenly over the corneal and conjunctival surfaces,,.


### Measurement


### Slit Lamp Setup:


### Magnification: Low to Medium ($6\times$ to $16\times$),.


### Filter: Insert the Cobalt Blue filter,.

Illumination: Use a wide beam (parallelepiped),,. Ensure the illumination is bright enough to make the green fluorescence visible.


### The Test:

Scan the cornea to ensure the tear film appears as a uniform green film,.

Instruct the patient to blink once and then keep their eyes wide open and stare straight ahead without blinking,,.

Observation: Watch the cornea through the slit lamp. Look for the appearance of black (dry) spots or streaks appearing within the green fluorescent film,,.

Timing: Begin counting (or start the stopwatch) immediately after the patient's last blink. Stop counting the moment the first dry spot appears,,.

Note: If the patient blinks before 10 seconds and before any dry spots appear, the trial is invalid and must be restarted,.

Repetition: Perform the test at least three times and calculate the average time,.


### 4. Interpretation of Results

Normal: A TBUT of 15 to 45 seconds is normal. Generally, anything greater than 10 seconds is considered normal,.

Unstable/Abnormal: A TBUT of less than 10 seconds suggests an unstable tear film and possible dry eye disease,,,.

Local Defect: If the dry spot appears in the exact same location during every trial, it likely indicates a defect in the corneal epithelium (surface texture) rather than a tear deficiency. This should not be counted as the TBUT,,.


### 5. Documentation (Recording Method)

Record the findings for each eye separately.


### Test Name: Record "TBUT" or "Tear Break-Up Time",.

Value: Record the time in seconds. It is best practice to record the average of the three trials,.


### Eye: Specify OD (Right) and OS (Left).


### Example Recording:

TBUT: OD 15 sec / OS 12 sec (Average).

TBUT: 8 sec OD (unstable).
