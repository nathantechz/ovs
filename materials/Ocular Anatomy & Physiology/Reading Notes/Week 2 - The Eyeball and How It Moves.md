# Ocular Anatomy & Physiology - Week 2: The Eyeball and How It Moves

## 1. The Globe: A Layered Ball

The eyeball is often called the **globe** . It isn't perfectly round like a marble; it’s actually made of two different curves joined together, but it is roughly the size of a quarter (about 1 inch wide). Only a small part of the eye (about one-sixth) shows on your face; the rest is hidden safely inside your head.

You can think of the eye as having three main layers, like a jawbreaker candy or a planet:

- **Layer 1: The Protective Layer (Outside).**  This is the tough outer skin. It includes the clear window (cornea) and the white shell (sclera).
- **Layer 2: The Nourishing Layer (Middle).**  This layer is full of blood vessels that carry food (nutrients) and oxygen to the eye parts. It is called the **Uvea** .
- **Layer 3: The Sensory Layer (Inside).**  This is the **Retina** . It acts like the film in a camera, catching the picture to send to your brain.

## 2. The Sclera and Episclera: The White Coat

- **The Sclera:**  This is the "white of the eye." It is a very strong, tough wall made of thick fibers (collagen) woven together. Its job is to protect the delicate insides of the eye and give the eye its shape.
- *Why is it white?*  The sclera is white because it is wet (hydrated). If you dried it out, it would actually become clear!.
- **The Episclera:**  Imagine a thin, loose clear skin on top of the white sclera. That is the episclera. It has blood vessels that help feed the sclera. It acts like a smooth lining to help the eye move around easily.

## 3. The Conjunctiva: The Clear Raincoat

The **Conjunctiva**  (pronounced *con-junk-tie-va* ) is a thin, clear skin that covers the front of the eye and lines the inside of your eyelids.

- **Two Parts:**
- **Bulbar:**  The part that covers the eyeball.
- **Palpebral:**  The part that lines the eyelids.
- **The "Sac":**  Because the conjunctiva connects the eye to the eyelid, it forms a pocket or a sac. This means a contact lens or an eyelash can never get lost behind your eye!.
- **Job:**  It makes mucus (slime) to keep the eye wet and slippery so blinking doesn't hurt. It also helps fight off germs.
- **Pink Eye:**  When this layer gets irritated or sick, the blood vessels get big and the eye looks pink. Doctors call this "conjunctivitis".

## 4. Extraocular Muscles: The Eye's Team of Movers

There are **six**  special muscles attached to the outside of each eye that control where you look. They work together to move the eye up, down, left, and right.

- **The Rectus Muscles (The Straight Ones):**  There are four of these. "Rectus" means straight.
- **Superior Rectus:**  Pulls the eye **up** .
- **Inferior Rectus:**  Pulls the eye **down** .
- **Medial Rectus:**  Pulls the eye **in**  toward your nose.
- **Lateral Rectus:**  Pulls the eye **out**  toward your ear.
- **The Oblique Muscles (The Slanted Ones):**  There are two of these. They help rotate the eye.
- **Superior Oblique:**  This is a cool muscle because it goes through a little hook or pulley made of cartilage called the **Trochlea**  before attaching to the eye.
- **Inferior Oblique:**  Starts at the bottom of the orbit and wraps around the eye.

**Working Together:**  When you look at something, your brain sends a signal to these muscles. If one muscle pulls, its partner has to relax so the eye can move smoothly. If the muscles don't work together perfectly, the eyes might cross or look in different directions. This is called **strabismus** .
