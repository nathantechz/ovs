# Neuro-Ophthalmology - Topic 1: Pupillary Reflex Pathways, RAPD, and Anisocoria Workup
## Doctor of Optometry &middot; Ocular Diseases Curriculum

---

## Overview & Objectives
Afferent pupillary defect (Marcus Gunn pupil), light-near dissociation, Horner's syndrome testing with apraclonidine, and Adie's tonic pupil.

## Core Concepts & Mechanisms

### Afferent Light Pathway
Retina → Optic Nerve → Chiasm → Optic Tract → Pretectal Nucleus (Midbrain) → bilateral Edinger-Westphal nuclei via posterior commissure.

### Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect (RAPD)
Swinging flashlight test shows paradoxical dilation of diseased eye when light moves from healthy to affected eye (indicates unilateral/asymmetric optic nerve disease or extensive retinal detachment).

### Anisocoria Evaluation
Greater anisocoria in dark = Sympathetic lesion (Horner's). Greater anisocoria in light = Parasympathetic lesion (CN III palsy or Adie's). Apraclonidine 0.5% reverses Horner anisocoria due to denervation supersensitivity of alpha-1 receptors.

## Clinical Pearls & Practice Links
> **Clinical Link:** A pupil-involving Third Nerve Palsy (fixed dilated pupil with ptosis and 'down and out' eye) is an emergency due to posterior communicating artery aneurysm.
