# Physical Optics - Week 2: Wave Parameters (The Anatomy of a Wave)

---

This week, we are looking at the specific parts that make up a wave. If you imagine watching ripples moving across a pond, or a jump rope being wiggled up and down, you are already picturing the concepts we use to describe light!

To understand how light works, scientists measure specific "parameters" (characteristics) of the wave. Here is your guide to the anatomy of a light wave:

## 1. Amplitude (A)

- **What it is:**  The "height" of the wave. If you draw a flat line through the exact middle of a wave, the amplitude is the distance from that flat line to the very top of a peak (the crest).
- **What it means for light:**  Amplitude is closely related to the *intensity*  or brightness of the light. A taller wave means a brighter light!

## 2. Wavelength (λ)

- **What it is:**  The physical distance between two matching parts of the wave. The easiest way to measure it is to take a tape measure and find the distance from the top of one peak to the top of the very next peak.
- **The Symbol:**  Scientists use the Greek letter lambda (λ) to represent wavelength.
- **What it means for light:**  In visible light, the wavelength determines the *color*  we see. Red light has a longer wavelength, while blue light has a shorter wavelength.

## 3. Frequency (f)

- **What it is:**  How many complete waves pass by a single point in one second. Imagine standing in the ocean and counting how many wave peaks hit you every second—that is the frequency!
- **What it means for light:**  Because light travels so fast, its frequency is incredibly high (trillions of waves per second).

## 4. Phase (φ)

- **What it is:**  Phase describes exactly where a wave is in its up-and-down cycle at a specific moment in time.
- **Why it matters:**  When two light waves meet, their phases determine how they interact. If their peaks perfectly line up, they are "in phase" and combine to make a bigger, brighter wave (constructive interference). If the peak of one wave lines up with the bottom (trough) of another, they are "out of phase" and can actually cancel each other out (destructive interference).

## 5. Velocity (v) and The Golden Rule of Waves (v = f \lambda)

- **What it is:**  Velocity is simply the speed at which the wave travels forward. In a vacuum, all electromagnetic waves (like light) travel at the exact same speed: the speed of light, usually written as c.
- **The Equation:**  The syllabus highlights a very important mathematical rule for all waves: **Velocity = Frequency × Wavelength**  (v = f\lambda).
- **The See-Saw Effect:**  Because the speed of light (v) stays the same in a vacuum, frequency and wavelength act like a see-saw. If a wave has a very long wavelength, it takes longer to pass by, so its frequency must be lower. If a wave has a very short wavelength, lots of them can pack into a single second, so its frequency goes way up!
