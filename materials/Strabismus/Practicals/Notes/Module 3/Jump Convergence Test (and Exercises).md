# Practical Clinical Guide: Jump Convergence Test (and Exercises)
## Strabismus &middot; Clinical Examination Protocol

---

## Clinical Procedure & Protocol

Equipment Needed: A distant detailed target (e.g., a letter on a 6-m test chart) and a near detailed accommodative target (e.g., a pencil or a small letter on a stick).


### I. Purpose and Principles

Clinical Value: This procedure evaluates the stamina, facility, and quality of the vergence system. Unlike the standard "push-up" Near Point of Convergence (NPC) which uses a smooth, gradual stimulus (a "ramp" stimulus), jump convergence demands a rapid, sudden change in vergence (a "step" stimulus).

Physiological Basis: It tests the visual system's ability to make large-scale, rapid disjunctive eye movements. It is considered a more difficult test than the standard push-up NPC because it requires highly accurate, immediate analysis of retinal disparity and physiological diplopia.


### II. Diagnostic Procedure

Establish Baseline: First, measure the patient's standard push-up NPC by bringing a target toward the nose until doubling occurs.

Position Targets: Have the patient fixate on the distant target (e.g., a letter at 6 meters). The examiner then positions the near target just a few centimeters further away than the patient's measured push-up NPC.

The "Jump": Instruct the patient to rapidly transfer their fixation from the distant target to the near target, attempting to make it single and clear immediately.

Alternation: Ask the patient to rapidly alternate fixation back and forth between the near and distant targets. Periodically check that the near target is being maintained close to their NPC.


### III. Clinical Observation & Interpretation

Normal Response: A patient with normal vergence facility will be able to make a single, smooth, and rapid change in convergence to successfully fuse the targets at both distances.

Abnormal Response (Convergence Insufficiency): Patients with poor convergence will struggle significantly with this "step" demand. You will observe them making several jerky, corrective adjustments to their angle of convergence rather than a single smooth movement, and they will frequently notice diplopia. In cases of convergence weakness exophoria, the "jump NPC" break point is often much further away (e.g., >20 cm) than their push-up NPC.

Compensatory Head Posture (Safety Warning): A very common, rapid reflex in patients struggling with this test is to suddenly pull their head backward to artificially increase the fixation distance. Clinical Pearl: Be very careful to ensure the patient does not bang their head on a hard chair headrest or wall behind them when they reflexively pull back.


### IV. Advanced Clinical Pearls: Therapeutic Application

Therapeutic Exercise: Jump convergence is not just a diagnostic test; it is a highly effective orthoptic exercise used to treat convergence insufficiency, often prescribed alongside or after the patient has mastered simple push-up exercises.

Adding Physiological Diplopia (Anti-Suppression): To ensure the patient is genuinely converging and not simply suppressing one eye during therapy, you can introduce a third target. Have the patient hold an additional target a few centimeters beyond the near target. As they jump their focus to the near target, they must simultaneously maintain awareness of the distal target, which should be seen in physiological diplopia (doubled). If they do not see the background target as doubled, suppression is occurring.
