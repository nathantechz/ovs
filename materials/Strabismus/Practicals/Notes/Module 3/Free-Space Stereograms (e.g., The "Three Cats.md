# Practical Clinical Guide: Free-Space Stereograms (e.g., The "Three Cats" & IFS Exercises)
## Strabismus &middot; Clinical Examination Protocol

---

## Clinical Procedure & Protocol

Equipment Needed: A printed free-space stereogram card (such as the "Three Cats" or Institute Free-Space Stereogram [IFS] cards) and a fixation target (such as a pencil, pen, or pin).


### I. Purpose and Principles

Clinical Value: Highly effective for treating convergence insufficiency, decompensated exophoria, and binocular instability by actively training the convergent (positive) fusional reserves. While primarily used for exophoric conditions, they can also be modified to treat esophoria with more difficulty.

Physiological Basis: Unlike simple push-up exercises, these stereograms actively dissociate the neurological link between convergence and accommodation. The patient must maintain a high level of convergence (to aim at the near pencil) while simultaneously relaxing their accommodation to focus clearly on the background card (exercising negative relative accommodation).


### II. Procedure (The "Three Cats" Method)

Positioning: Have the patient hold the card containing two incomplete cats at arm's length (or reading distance) at eye level.

Fixation: The patient holds a pencil halfway between their eyes and the card and focuses intently on the pencil.

Physiological Diplopia: Because the patient is focused on the near pencil, the background card is seen in physiological diplopia, causing the two background cats to appear as four blurred images.

Fusion: Instruct the patient to adjust the distance of the pencil back and forth until the middle two cat images overlap and fuse. The resulting percept will be of three cats: a complete cat in the center, flanked by two incomplete cats.

Clearing the Image: Once three cats are seen, ask the patient to try to make the cats look clear. This is the crucial therapeutic step, as it forces the patient to relax their accommodation while maintaining their over-converged posture.

III. Clinical Progression & Therapy Regimen To continually build fusional stamina and voluntary convergence, the exercise should be made progressively harder:

Step 1: Once the patient can steadily maintain the three cats using a pencil, swap the pencil for a much smaller target, like a pin or needle.

Step 2: The patient practices quickly removing the pin while attempting to maintain the fused "three cats" image without a physical target to aim at.

Step 3: While maintaining the three cats without a pin, the patient slowly advances the card closer to their nose. At this stage, the patient is utilizing voluntary convergence.

Dosage: These exercises are typically prescribed to be performed at home for 10 minutes, twice a day. A follow-up appointment should be scheduled shortly after, typically in 3 to 4 weeks, to monitor progress and maintain motivation.


### IV. Advanced Clinical Pearls

Built-in Suppression Checks: You must ensure the patient is actually fusing and not just suppressing one eye. Choose stereogram targets that have unique control marks on each half of the pair, or targets that provide stereoscopic relief (a 3-D effect). If a unique mark disappears or the 3-D depth is lost, the patient is suppressing.

Institute Free-Space Stereograms (IFS): Because patients can rapidly become bored with the "Three Cats," the IFS system was developed. It provides a booklet of varied exercises using 3-D floating rings and "magic-eye" style autostereograms to maintain interest, particularly in children.

Step vs. Ramp Stimuli: The most effective therapies use varied demands. Free-space stereograms can be used as a "ramp" exercise (slowly moving the card toward the nose while maintaining fusion) or a "step" exercise (rapidly shifting the eyes down a page of targets that are spaced progressively further apart to demand instant, large jumps in convergence)
