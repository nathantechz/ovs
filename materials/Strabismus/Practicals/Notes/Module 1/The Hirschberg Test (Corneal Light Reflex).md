# Practical Clinical Guide: The Hirschberg Test (Corneal Light Reflex)
## Strabismus &middot; Clinical Examination Protocol

---

## Clinical Procedure & Protocol


### Equipment Needed: A pen-torch light.


### Procedure:

Shine a pen-torch light at the patient's eyes from a distance of 50 to 100 cm.

Instruct the patient to fixate directly on the light.

Crucial technique: The examiner must observe the corneal reflexes using their dominant eye positioned directly behind the light source.


### Clinical Value & Interpretation:

Purpose: It is a rapid method to assess ocular alignment and estimate the angle of strabismus, though it is generally considered less accurate than cover testing.

Measurement: Observe the displacement of the corneal reflex from the center of the pupil.

Conversion: While some authorities state that 1 mm of displacement is equivalent to 12 prism diopters (12Δ), others suggest it equates to approximately 22Δ of deviation.


### Important Clinical Considerations:

Angle Kappa: It is vital to account for angle kappa when interpreting the results. A perfectly centered reflex (a zero angle) is rare; it is normal to have a physiological displacement of the pupil reflex of about +0.5 to +1.0 mm.

Sensitivity Limits: The realistic sensitivity of the Hirschberg test is limited to about 5Δ. This is because it is nearly impossible for an examiner to visually detect a reflex displacement of less than 0.25 mm.

Improving Accuracy: To add accuracy to this estimation, you can perform the related Krimsky test, which utilizes a prism bar to optically centralize the displaced corneal reflex.
