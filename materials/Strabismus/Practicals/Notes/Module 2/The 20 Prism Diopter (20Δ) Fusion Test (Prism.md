# Practical Clinical Guide: The 20 Prism Diopter (20Δ) Fusion Test (Prism Reflex Test)
## Strabismus &middot; Clinical Examination Protocol

---

## Clinical Procedure & Protocol

Equipment Needed: A 15Δ or 20Δ loose prism and a detailed near fixation target.


### I. Purpose and Principles

Clinical Value: This is a rapid, objective screening test used to evaluate the patient's ability to make a fusional vergence movement to maintain Binocular Single Vision (BSV).

Pediatric Application: Because it requires no verbal input, it is an excellent tool for assessing motor fusion in pre-verbal children (6 months of age and older) and patients who cannot cooperate with subjective sensory tests.

Physiological Basis: Placing a base-out prism in front of an eye shifts the retinal image nasally. If normal sensory and motor fusion are intact, this disparity immediately triggers a reflex vergence movement (adduction) to place the image back onto the fovea and prevent diplopia.


### II. Procedure

Instruct the patient to fixate on the detailed near target.

Rapidly introduce the 20Δ prism base-out (base oriented horizontally towards the ear) in front of the right eye.

Carefully observe the movements of both the eye under the prism and the fellow eye.

Remove the prism and allow a brief pause for the patient's fusion to stabilize.

Repeat the test by placing the prism base-out in front of the left eye.


### III. Clinical Observation & Interpretation

Normal Response (BSV is Present): To maintain single vision, the eyes must converge. You will typically observe a horizontal version movement of both eyes moving away from the side of the prism, followed immediately by a fusional vergence (adduction) movement of the eye not behind the prism to take up fixation.

Quality Check: Beyond simply noting if the eyes move inward, assess the number of movements, the smoothness, and the speed of re-taking fixation to gauge the overall quality and robustness of the patient's fusion.


### Abnormal Response (BSV is Absent / Suppression):

Prism over the Non-Fixing (Suppressed) Eye: If the prism is placed in front of an eye that is already suppressing, the image shift is not perceived by the brain, and no movement of either eye will be observed.

Prism over the Fixing Eye: If the prism is placed in front of the dominant fixing eye, both eyes will make the initial conjugate version movement to follow the image (away from the prism), but no corrective adduction movement will occur to regain binocularity.


### IV. Advanced Clinical Pearls

Target Selection: Always use a detailed accommodative target (like a small picture or letter) rather than a simple penlight. A detailed target provides the necessary stimulus to lock accommodation and convergence, whereas a plain light might not provide enough contour detail to stimulate a strong fusional reflex.

Bilateral Testing is Mandatory: Because the test will yield different abnormal responses depending on whether the prism is placed over the fixing or non-fixing eye, you must perform the test over each eye separately to accurately detect unilateral suppression
