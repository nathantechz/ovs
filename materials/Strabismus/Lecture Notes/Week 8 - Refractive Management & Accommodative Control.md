## I. Physiological Basis: The Link Between Accommodation and Vergence

## 1. The AC/A Ratio

- **Definition:**  The Accommodative Convergence to Accommodation (AC/A) ratio expresses the relationship between the amount of convergence elicited per diopter of accommodation.
- **Mechanism:**  Because of Hering’s Law of Equal Innervation, the impulse to accommodate (focus at near) automatically sends an impulse to converge.
- **High AC/A:**  A unit of accommodation triggers an abnormally large amount of convergence. This can cause **convergence excess esotropia**  (esotropia at near).
- **Low AC/A:**  Accommodation triggers very little convergence, potentially leading to **convergence insufficiency**  or exophoria at near.

## 2. Refractive Modification Principles

- **Plus Lenses:**  Relax accommodation. Because accommodation is relaxed, accommodative convergence decreases. This is used to treat **eso-deviations**  (in-turning eyes).
- **Minus Lenses:**  Stimulate accommodation. This drives accommodative convergence. This is used to treat **exo-deviations**  (out-turning eyes).

## II. Management of Accommodative Esotropia

## 1. Refractive Accommodative Esotropia

- **Etiology:**  Uncorrected hypermetropia (farsightedness) forces the patient to exert excessive accommodation to clear the retinal image. This excessive accommodation drives excessive convergence. If fusional divergence is insufficient to compensate, esotropia results.
- **Primary Treatment:**  The standard of care is **full refractive correction**  of the hyperopia, often determined by a cycloplegic refraction.
- **Mechanism:**  Glasses clear the retinal image without the need for accommodation. When accommodation relaxes, the associated convergence relaxes, and the eyes straighten.
- **Clinical Goal:**  In fully accommodative esotropia, the deviation is completely eliminated by the glasses.

## 2. Non-Refractive Accommodative Esotropia (High AC/A)

- **Presentation:**  The eyes are straight at distance but crossing (esotropic) at near.
- **Treatment:**
- **Bifocals:**  Prescribing bifocals (often +2.50 or +3.00 add) eliminates the need to accommodate at near. This prevents the excessive convergence response associated with the high AC/A ratio.
- **Miotics:**  Pharmaceutical agents (e.g., phospholine iodide) can be used to reduce the AC/A ratio peripherally, though side effects limit their use.

## 3. Partial (Decompensated) Accommodative Esotropia

- **Definition:**  Refractive correction reduces the angle of deviation, but a manifest esotropia remains (the non-accommodative portion).
- **Management:**  Surgery is generally indicated for the *non-accommodative*  portion of the deviation only. The accommodative portion must still be managed with spectacles.

## III. Management of Exodeviations

## 1. Intermittent Exotropia and Convergence Insufficiency

- **Refractive Correction:**  Full correction of myopia is essential. Unlike esotropes, patients with exotropia who are hyperopic may be **under-corrected**  (given less plus) to stimulate accommodation and convergence.
- **Over-Minusing (Negative Adds):**
- **Strategy:**  Prescribing concave (minus) lenses—or "over-minusing" a patient—stimulates accommodation. This drives accommodative convergence, which helps control the exodeviation.
- **Application:**  This is often used for intermittent exotropia or divergence excess exotropia, particularly in younger patients with active accommodation.

## IV. Modification Strategies and "Weaning"

## 1. Modifying Spheres for Heterophoria

- **Concept:**  Small spherical additions or subtractions can be used to manipulate the vergence system to treat decompensated heterophoria.
- **For Esophoria:**  A plus addition at near (e.g., +1.00D) reduces accommodation and helps relieve the esophoria.
- **For Exophoria:**  A minus addition (or reducing the plus reading add) stimulates convergence to help control the exophoria.

## 2. Reducing Compensatory Lenses (Weaning)

- **Bifocals in Esotropia:**  If bifocals are used for high AC/A esotropia, they are typically fitted high (bisecting the pupil). As the child grows and fusional vergence amplitudes improve, the goal is often to "wean" the patient out of the bifocal or reduce the power of the add, provided binocularity can be maintained.
- **Contact Lenses:**  In cases of anisometropia (unequal refractive error), contact lenses are often superior to spectacles for maintaining binocularity because they minimize aniseikonia (image size differences) and prismatic effects.

## V. Summary of Clinical Rules (Week 8)

- **Esotropia:**  Suspect hypermetropia. If found (via cycloplegic refraction), prescribe **full plus** . If esotropia remains at near only, consider **bifocals**  (High AC/A).
- **Exotropia:**  Correct myopia fully. Consider **reducing plus**  (under-correcting hyperopia) or **over-minusing**  to stimulate convergence.
- **Binocularity First:**  The primary goal of refractive management in these cases is to restore or maintain binocular single vision (BSV). Surgery is usually reserved for deviations that cannot be corrected refractively.
